**Software engineering is not dead. It is evolving into something much bigger.**

I came across an interesting post about the current state of Physical AI, and it got me thinking about where software engineering is heading.

For years, much of our industry has revolved around web development, applications, APIs, and traditional infrastructure. AI is already changing how much of that software gets written, and I suspect some of those traditional development roles will become less relevant over time.

But I don't believe software engineering is going away. Quite the opposite.

I see Physical AI, robotics, and intelligent automation opening an entirely new chapter for engineers.

Three areas stand out to me:

- **Physical AI and robotics:** We are moving beyond AI generating text, images, and code. We are entering a period where software interacts directly with the physical world. Agriculture, manufacturing, logistics, mining, and industrial maintenance are already seeing practical applications. These systems will require engineers who understand software, hardware integration, and real-world constraints.

- **Simulation, testing, and validation:** A robot performing a task in a demonstration is one thing. Having that same robot perform reliably thousands of times in unpredictable environments is another. Simulation environments, automated testing, regression analysis, and continuous validation will become increasingly important. This is where traditional software engineering practices have a significant role to play.

- **Infrastructure, deployment, and reliability:** Physical AI will need much more than sophisticated models. These systems will require build pipelines, version control, CI/CD, monitoring, deployment strategies, failure recovery, and long-term maintenance. A robot that works 95% of the time might make an impressive demonstration, but that does not necessarily make it ready for production.

What interests me most is how much of this future depends on engineering disciplines we have been practicing for decades.

The tools will change. The environments will change. The problems will become more complex.

But the fundamentals of software engineering are not disappearing.

**We may be moving away from simply building applications toward building the intelligence and infrastructure that operate in the physical world.**

And personally, I find that direction far more interesting.
